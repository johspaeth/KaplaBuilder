# Rule lab: 21 kid requests vs the physics checker

Goal: turn requests like "Papa, I want a Kapla duck that stands on one leg, in a tree" into real plank plans of at most 200 planks, run every step through the physics checker, and see which rules break and why.

This folder is not a component. It imports `KaplaPhysics`, changes nothing in `core/`, `physics/`, `specs/` or `comparator/`, and is not in `defaultTargets`, so CI does not build it.

## Run it

```sh
lake build kaplalab
.lake/build/bin/kaplalab > lab/results/lab-results.json            # all 21 designs, about 9.5 min
.lake/build/bin/kaplalab duck200 duck_one_leg                       # some designs; summary on stderr
python3 lab/gen_duck200.py /tmp/duck200.lean lab/results/duck200-parts.json   # regenerate the 200-plank duck
python3 lab/to_blueprint.py lab/results/lab-results.json lab/results/duck200-parts.json lab/blueprints
lake exe kapla view lab/blueprints/duck200.json --out out/duck200    # open out/duck200/index.html
```

`gen_duck200.py` writes the Lean list `duck200Steps` (paste it into `KaplaLab.lean`) and one part label per plank. The list in `KaplaLab.lean` is the generator's output, unchanged.

## How the designs were built

- Every design is a plan: a list of planks in building order, written by hand in `KaplaLab.lean` with a few helpers (`tower`, `leaningTower`, `deckX`, one constructor per pose).
- The 200-plank duck is generated by `gen_duck200.py`. It places the tree, then orders the duck's planks so that the weight stays over the single leg (worst offset 0.86 of the allowed 1.5 units along x, and 0.30 of 0.5 along y).
- Each design was run through the checker and fixed until the remaining failures were caused by the rules, not by the design. The fixes for the 200-plank duck are listed in its section below.
- No planner or semantic engine was used. These designs test the rules, not the request parser or the search.

## Setup

- Checker: `KaplaPhysics` on `main` at `446591f`. The same designs gave identical results, step by step, on the PR #14 engine at `4a2dc0a`.
- Plank: 15 × 3 × 1 units, 1 unit = 7.8 mm (11.7 × 2.34 × 0.78 cm, the real Kapla size: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapla). The standard Kapla box holds 200 planks (https://www.kapla.com/en/kapla-200-box_p37.html), which matches the 200-plank cap.
- For every step k of every plan, the lab records:
  - whether the first k planks are well-formed (no overlaps, every plank resting on something);
  - whether they stand exactly (`stabilityAnswer`);
  - whether they are certified solid at 2 mm and at 1 mm placement error (`solidAnswer`);
  - which straight path places the plank (`reachDir?`).
- Each finished build was also checked at 0, 0.5, 1, 1.87 and 1.95 mm, together with `checkPlan {} (10/39) 200` (clearance off, the default) and `hardToReach`.
- Files:
  - `KaplaLab.lean`: all designs, written as code, and the runner (`lake build kaplalab`).
  - `gen_duck200.py`: generator for the 200-plank duck.
  - `to_blueprint.py`: turns results into blueprints for `kapla view` (web/README.md).
  - `results/lab-results.json`: full per-step results. `results/duck200-parts.json`: the part of each duck plank.
  - `blueprints/*.json`: one blueprint per design. Each step card shows what the checker says after that step.

## Findings

### 1. The 2 mm error sits on a cliff

At 1.95 mm (exactly 1/4 unit), every finished build that is risky at 2 mm becomes solid. At 2 mm (10/39 unit, about 0.256), they all become risky again. The finished-build verdicts are identical at 0.5, 1, 1.87 and 1.95 mm.

The reason: a standing or on-edge plank has a contact face 1 unit (7.8 mm) wide. Two planks in contact can each be off by ε in opposite directions, so the load can move by 2ε relative to the face below. The checker needs 2ε of margin on both sides, so the face must be at least 4ε wide. That holds only up to ε = 1/4 unit, which is 1.95 mm. The spec's ε = 2 mm is 0.05 mm past that line. As a result, no plank resting on a 1-unit face can ever be certified at 2 mm.

### 2. "Risky" mixes two different things

R4 says "Risky" means "some small misplacement could make it fall". R3 allows the checker to be cautious, so in practice "Risky" means only "could not be certified". In these builds the two separate cleanly.

Genuine risk, where a worst-case 2 mm error can really tip the build (my analysis, not proved in Lean):
- the one-leg duck's body on the leg top;
- the giraffe's head on its standing-plank neck;
- the spoiler wing on its post (both race cars: on-edge post or standing post).

In each case, everything above a 1-unit face has its center of mass exactly over the face's center line. Two 2 mm errors in opposite directions move it 4 mm, but the half-face is only 3.9 mm. The failure margin is 0.1 mm.

Checker gap, where the build is solid in reality but cannot be certified:
- a beam on standing legs (puppy, giraffe);
- a chassis on on-edge axles (box car, and the first risky step of both race cars);
- on-edge bridge rails;
- the duck's "strong leg";
- a single standing plank on a plank.

The argument (by hand, not proved in Lean): a vertical plank loaded only from above, on a face that lies inside its own footprint, cannot tip. Its base is the same rectangle as its top. The certificate still requires each load point to stay 2ε from the edges of a 1-unit face, which is impossible at 2 mm.

- R5 already says planks pushed against a neighbour move with it. The strong-leg duck's three legs touch side by side, so R5 should tie them together. The engine does not implement R5. It treats every plank's error separately, which is sound but more cautious than needed.
- The flat-axle car shows the same thing from the other side: the same car with 3-unit-wide axle contacts is solid at every step.

### 3. Build order: one plank per step is too strict for some animals

- Child's order: the duck built as the child describes it (body, neck, head, then tail) really falls for three steps, because the neck and head go on before the tail counterweight. A reordered plan (body, one tail feather, neck, tail, head, tail) stands at every step. The planner can solve this kind of problem by reordering.
- Duck on a branch: the branch balances on one leaf plank. The finished build is solid at 2 mm. But the counterweight alone tips the branch backward, and the duck alone tips it forward. No one-plank-at-a-time order exists. A real builder places the two together, or holds one of them. Under K1 (one plank per step) and K4 (every step must stand), this design cannot be built.

### 4. Things a child will ask for that the rules cannot express (B4)

B4 allows only six axis-aligned poses, so these were not attempted:
- round wheels;
- a beak or windshield at an angle;
- a spiral staircase (each plank turned a few degrees);
- a sloped roof;
- a true arch.

The corbel arch shows the usual workaround: planks stepped in 2-unit offsets. It is solid. The other requests need a translation step ("wheels become on-edge axles") that tells the child what changed.

### 5. Reachability (K3) never got in the way, and is too generous in one case

Every plank was reachable from above or from the side. The car driver plank, slid into a 3-unit gap under the roof with zero clearance, was accepted as reachable from +y. That matches the PR #14 review comment about sliding face contact. (The clearance check, now off by default, is a different rule: it is about gaps that errors could close, not about the path in.) If K3 changes at all, it should get stricter (require a little clearance), not looser.

### 6. 200 planks are not a problem

A 100-level square tower (200 planks, 78 cm) is solid at every step. The compiled checker needed about 3 minutes for all 200 steps (each step checked for well-formedness, exact stability, and certification at 2 mm and 1 mm). All 21 designs take about 9.5 minutes in total. The time is fine for a demo, but too slow for live typing. Interpreted Lean is much slower.

## The 200-plank duck

Request: "Papa, I want a Kapla duck that stands on 1 leg, in a tree. Use all 200 blocks."

Exactly 200 planks, 75 cm tall. The generator is `gen_duck200.py`, and the plan is the design `duck200` in `KaplaLab.lean`. To see it: `lake exe kapla view lab/blueprints/duck200.json --out out/duck200`.

| Part | Planks | How it is built |
|---|---|---|
| Trunk | 104 | 52-level square crib tower, 41 cm |
| Canopy and leaves | 17 | 5-plank deck, then a 6-level pinwheel of leaves sticking out 6 units in turn |
| Leg | 1 | one standing plank (3 × 1 footprint) in the middle of the canopy |
| Body | 41 | keel, cross beam, then a crib of alternating long and cross layers, narrow at the bottom |
| Wings | 5 | the top body layer pushed 3 units back; they also carry the tail |
| Tail | 10 | two prongs rising backward 1.5 units per level |
| Neck | 13 | a crossing column of single planks at the front of the body |
| Head, beak, crest | 9 | a small crib on the neck top: beak 6 units out front, a 6-unit-wide crest centred over the neck |

Checker results:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Stands at exact positions | all 200 steps |
| Finished build at 2 mm | risky (the one-plank leg, finding 2) |
| Finished build at 1.95 mm, 1 mm, 0.5 mm | solid |
| Steps certified at 2 mm | 121 of 200: the tree, then nothing once the leg (step 121) is on |
| Steps certified at 1 mm | 196 of 200 |
| Hard to reach | none |
| Run time | about 1 min 40 s for all 200 steps |

What it took to get there (each attempt run through the checker):

1. **Tail tipped its support.** The first version fell from step 180. The back plank that carried the tail rested on only 9 units of the body, and the tail pulled it over the back edge. The back planks moved forward and the tail now rises more steeply.
2. **Body rows tipped separately.** The second version got stuck at step 172 and later fell. Each of the five rows of body planks rested only on the narrow cross beam, so the neck tipped the front row forward on its own. The body became a crib, with cross layers tying the rows together.
3. **Beak tipped the head.** The third version fell at the beak. A head of one long plank on a 3-unit-wide neck top cannot carry a beak 6 units out front. The head became a small crib, with the crest as a counterweight.
4. **Crest pulled the head off-centre.** The fourth version stood at every step but was risky even at 0.5 mm, because the crest pulled the head 1.2 units off the neck centre. Centring the crest made the finished build solid at 1 mm.

The 4 steps that are still not solid at 1 mm (131, 141, 181, 195; the viewer shows them as 132, 142, 182, 196) are all the same thing: one plank of a mirror pair is on and its partner is not yet. On a 1-unit-wide leg, that moment uses most of the sideways margin. A two-hand step (recommendation 4) would remove all four.

So, with 200 planks, the duck on one leg in a tree can be built today one plank at a time without anything falling. It only fails the plan check because of the 2 mm cliff (finding 1). At 1.95 mm, with the two-hand step for the 4 mirror pairs, it would pass the full plan check.

Caveat: this last claim is inferred from the per-step results at 1 mm and at 1.95 mm. I did not run the full plan check at 1.95 mm, and I did not test pair placement, because the checker places one plank per step.

## What to relax, and what not to

These are proposals for the owners of `spec/` and `physics/`. This folder changes none of them.

1. **Fix the checker before relaxing physics.**
   - Add a proved prism rule: a vertical plank loaded only from above, within its own top face, never tips.
   - Implement R5 so touching planks share their error.
   - By my analysis, together these would turn 5 of the 11 risky designs into solid (bridge, strong-leg duck, puppy, both box cars), with no change to the rules.
2. **Decide ε deliberately.**
   - Keep 2 mm, and accept that nothing can ever be certified solid on a 1-unit face.
   - Or set ε = 1/4 unit (1.95 mm), the largest value at which those faces can be certified.
   - Or use a separate, smaller ε for standing and on-edge planks, which builders place with more care.
   - Whichever you choose, state the cliff in the spec. Right now a 0.05 mm change flips 11 of the other 20 designs, and the 200-plank duck.
3. **Split "Risky" into two answers:** "could fall" (with a witness misplacement, for example 2 mm left on the leg and 2 mm right on everything above) and "not certified". The PR #14 review already asked for ¬Solid witnesses.
4. **Allow a two-hand step (relax K1, check K4 after the pair):** two planks placed at the same time, checked only after both are down. Core already lets a step hold up to `hands` blocks, but `CoreAdapter.lean` checks the build after every block, and says a step whose blocks only stand together is refused. Checking after every core step instead is the smallest change that makes counterweighted branches and cantilevers buildable, and it would also remove the 4 mirror-pair steps of the 200-plank duck. Temporary props that are removed later are a much bigger change, since plans would then need removal steps.
5. **Keep B4 for v1.** Have the request-to-design layer map angled or round requests to axis-aligned stand-ins and say so ("I made the wheels flat planks on edge").
6. **Tighten K3 slightly** to require clearance instead of allowing face-sliding.

## Caveats

- Results are for `main` at `446591f`. A later engine change can change them: rerun `kaplalab`.
- "Risky" verdicts are not proofs of failure. The genuine-risk and checker-gap labels in finding 2 are my analysis, not Lean theorems.
- These are hand-built designs, not the semantic engine's output. They test the rules, not the request parser.
